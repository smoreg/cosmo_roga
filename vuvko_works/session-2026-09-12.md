## Key technical decisions

- Flat-top axial hex lattice, turned a sixth, column 0 on the keel:
  `x = originX + 1.5R·q`, `y = originY + ft·(r + q/2)`, `R = ft/√3`,
  origin `(shipWidth/2, 0)`.
- Wesnoth combat: adjacent-only, "ranged" = a weapon class that avoids counter,
  ZOC, alternating strikes, 75% independent per strike.
- Commands-in/events-out pure reducer; counter-based RNG so replay is exact;
  a match is `{seed, commands}`.
- Named event constructors in `src/core/events.ts` — never inline literals.
- Custom ESLint rules `house/no-wrapper-lambda`, `house/no-duplicate-lambda`.
- `eslint-plugin-boundaries` walling `src/core` off from React/DOM.
- typescript-eslint `strictTypeChecked`, `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`,
  `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`, `verbatimModuleSyntax`.
- Vitest projects (core=node, components=jsdom), 90/85 coverage on `src/core`.
- Container queries, not media queries.
- Geomorph pipeline: `layout()` → raster (PX_FT=4) → flood regions → contacts →
  stubs → pickDoors → dressUp.

# Part 2 — this session, in order

## Rendering fixes

**The deck plan had shrunk, not faded.** `renderBlueprint` computed a tile's
size as pixel-width ÷ 12 — the *source* resolution — while what ships is a
4 px/ft bake. Every tile drew at 1/6 scale. The bake now writes `atlas.json` and
the renderer reads it. `tileGeometry()` is pure so it can be tested without a
canvas. — `55328a2`

**The blur was measured in the wrong unit.** The backdrop's radius was a
fraction of a hex, so at 35 ft hexes it smeared artwork whose features are about
a foot across. Now 0.7 ft, in feet of ship. Also found: a tile's footprint is
what the tile *declares*, not the image less a fixed bleed — an eighth of the
library bleeds unevenly. — `97394ab`

**Tile size now comes from the index, never the downloaded file.** Twice the
plan drew at the wrong scale because the renderer inferred feet from pixels in
hand. `px / 12` from the index is correct at every bake resolution. — `38144d2`

**Lattice weight** raised so it holds against the now-legible plan: edges gain
colour and width, fill 0.42 → 0.6 → 0.75 at request. — `dea8497`, `7a995a2`

**A browser screenshot harness** (`scripts/screenshot.mjs`) — generation needs a
canvas, so the drawing stage was the one part never tested.

## 20 ft hexes

Switched from 35 ft to 20 ft. Two rules turned out to be secretly measured in
hexes and were converted:

- Movement 4 was really **140 ft**; left alone a turn would have got a third as
  far. Now `movementFeet`, divided by the deck's hex size → move 7 at 20 ft.
- "A node needs 4 hexes of room" was really **~4,200 sq ft**; left alone it
  would have tripled the ship's income. — `b704cff`

## The design documents

Five research streams were run (tabletop encounter budgets, video-game director
budgets, PCG constraint literature, extraction/hazard design, and a
Slay-the-Spire/Darkest-Dungeon pass). Three documents came out of it:

**`design/rscore.md`** — the budget that decides what a derelict carries.
Capacity is summed from the extracted deck, not predicted from the hull (every
pre-generation predictor was too weak: tiled floor r=0.49, sections r=0.40,
bounding rectangle r=0.27). The pool buys what takes a turn — PF2e prices a
complex hazard as a same-level monster and a simple one at one fifth. Relief is
bought at a positive price; no surveyed system implements a negative cost. Spent
during placement, never checked after, because a budget is a global counting
constraint and greedy local placers handle those badly.
— `83a9802`, `f3d2a1f`, `d1e7d96`

**`design/alert-and-age.md`** — Cogmind's alert plus Duskers' age. Alert is the
ship noticing; age is the ship dying. Age is not a third purse: it decides how
the one pool splits, so a Fatal hull holds little production and therefore
cannot act on the alert it reaches. Influence is tracked in three streams
(percussive, structural, observed) and the dominant one decides what the ship
sends. — `1505027`

**`design/interactables.md`** — a spawn zone and its nodes are one purchase, a
*plant*, because neither is worth anything alone; that makes two of the four
degenerate missions impossible by construction. Seclusion prices position.
Power is the lock and it is shared: the lights you need are the lights the ship
needs. A threat in an unpowered section costs a third, so the player completes
the purchase later. — `4f6cdd9`

## Generation problems

**Only 191 of 1,789 tiles could ever be placed** (10.7%), because the profile
hull cuts only 100×100 slots and `rim: "none"` removes every 50×50 and 100×50
one. Entire kinds were unreachable: bridge 560/560, corner 302/302, edge
231/231.

**Fuel plumbing, overlays, and the same-index rule** — `41b832c`
- Tiles named Intake or Scoop excluded: 1789 → 1594 base tiles.
- Overlays had never shipped — 0 of 1789 — because the index kept only
  taxonomy-classified tiles and an overlay has no geometry to classify. A base
  tile is nearly empty: E510 "Cargo Bay" is a blank shell at 15% ink. 445
  overlays now ride along.
- An overlay belongs to the same index **in the same folder**: 66 of 977 codes
  span more than one directory, and 16 overlays could have crossed.
- Selection was broken: it picked an overlay then discarded it on size mismatch.

**Walls and doors read off the art** — `97066a6`
- The taxonomy could not tell a hull wall from an interior wall and fell back to
  the folder name, so 135 of the 143 fully walled 50 ft rooms were stamped as
  hull corners.
- The discriminator is the door: a side with a door cannot face space.
- Finding doors needed **luminance, not alpha** — the archive draws a wall as a
  white line and a door as a black break in it, both fully opaque.
- The convention: **one door at the middle of every 50 ft module of an edge** —
  25 ft on a 50 ft side, 25 and 75 on a 100 ft side. Off-convention under 2%.

**Fifty foot sections** — `4f6edd4` (WIP)
- A profile digit now counts a 50 ft section. 16–24 sections per hull instead of
  4–6. Caps stay 100×100.
- Parity enforced: rows are centred, so a row whose width does not match the
  beam's parity lands 25 ft off grid. Phase is computed, not accumulated.
- **Relaxed fits fell from ~7 per hull to 0–2.**

## Corrections I made during the session

Worth keeping because they were each argued from data:

1. Claimed the archive had 3 interior 50×50 tiles. It was reading a *folder
   convention*, not the art; the real figure is ~143 fully walled.
2. Claimed "0 interior slots at 100 ft bays" as evidence the library wants a
   50 ft grid. True but misleading — the zero came from the half-bay offset, and
   the tile pools say the opposite (100×100 is 65% interior, 50×50 only 7%).
3. Claimed doors were underivable from the art. They are derivable; I had
   sampled the alpha channel, where an opaque white wall and an opaque black
   door are identical.

## Two things are broken

**1. The transition piece.** It is modelled as a row inserted between two
others, but the six 100×50 "100' to 50' Transition Corridor" tiles declare
`attach: ["s"]` with `skin: ["e","n","w"]` and `class: cap`. They are bow
pieces; the narrowing happens inside the tile and its narrow end is the nose.
Their own doors say so — two at 25 and 75 ft on the wide side, one at 50 ft on
the narrow. Those slots currently take whatever 100×50 tile fits, which is tram
stations.

*Fix:* make it a **cap substitution** — a hull whose bow row is two sections
wide may take a nose transition instead of the square 100×100 cap.

**2. The profile table.** `src/core/missions.ts` still names `1-2-1`, `1-2-3`,
`2-1-2`, `3-2-1`, which under the new section meaning are quarter-size ships.
They need doubling in both axes and checking for parity — all-odd or all-even
digits. Tests pass because none asserts a hull's size; one should.
