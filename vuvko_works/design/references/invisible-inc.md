# Invisible, Inc. — the alarm, and everything hanging off it

Research dump, 2026-09-09. Klei, 2015. Sources: the Game Developer deep dive by
the team, James Lantz's GDC 2015 talk, Tom Francis's essay, the wikis.

## The alarm was renamed, and that was a design fix

It shipped as **ALARM** and became **SECURITY LEVEL** in the UI — described by the
team as a "small but incredibly effective change" that "provided contextual
framing that reduced player frustration about unfairness." Same numbers; the word
*alarm* implied the player had erred, and *security level* implied the building
was simply doing its job.

<https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-alarm-systems-in-klei-s-i-invisible-inc-i->

The counter was also collapsed from an original **20–30 granular levels to 6
visible levels of 5 sub-ticks each** — 30 ticks under the hood, six bands on the
screen — specifically so consequences would be predictable enough to plan around.
Lantz, on the priority order: *"our goal was not to make the alarm levels
balanced… our goal was to create movie-like tension and drama above all else."*

**Legibility outranked balance.** That is the sentence to keep.

## What raises it

- A passive tick every turn. The clock always moves; this is the anti-turtle spine.
- Killing a guard: +1 via heart monitor, +2 for advanced monitors.
- Knocking a guard out, where they carry a consciousness monitor: +1.
- A guard finding a body, conscious or not.
- A camera spotting an agent: +1 per turn spotted.

## What each band does

| Level | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Extra cameras activate — one turn of grace before they are live |
| 2 | **Every firewall gains +1 strength** — taxes the hacking economy directly |
| 3 | An additional guard patrol enters the building |
| 4 | Another patrol; cameras reboot; guard vision range and arc get buffed |
| 5–6 | Enforcers (elite, armoured) spawn; agents get located on the map; daemons compound; mobility and armour stack on remaining guards |

Six is the base-game cap; Endless Mode continues the same ramp. Note that no two
bands do the same *kind* of thing — one touches vision, one touches the hacking
economy, one adds bodies. An escalation ladder that only ever adds enemies is a
worse ladder.

<https://invisibleinc.fandom.com/wiki/Alarm_Levels>

## Why the alarm exists

Klei studied XCOM, FTL and Spelunky and named the missing ingredient as "a sense
of time pressure, either in certain parts or throughout." The reasoning is
mechanical: a perfect-information stealth game with peek and predict tools
*degenerates* without an external clock — you scout everything, you wait out every
patrol, and the analysis paralysis is unbounded. The alarm is the replacement for
an AI director: a clock that is also an antagonist, escalating regardless of how
well you play, so that correct play is **timed** rather than merely accurate.

The other non-negotiable was total information about guards. Klei had shipped a
stealth game where "the guards' intentions were transparent and readable" and kept
that. Tom Francis: enemy vision renders as literal light, so the player gets
"perfect, reliable, hard-and-fast intel." The alarm's cost has to be legible for
exactly the same reason the vision cone is.

## Turn structure and combat

- Movement costs AP: 1 per orthogonal tile, √2 ≈ 1.41 per diagonal.
- **Peeking through a door and predicting a guard's next move each cost 1 AP** —
  information is metered in the same currency as movement. Every turn is a
  scout-or-advance trade.
- Guards see a **90° cone**, split into *watched* (reacts instantly) and *noticed*
  (will glance over and investigate) zones.
- **Overwatch**: a guard who spots an agent locks a watch on that tile. You have
  exactly 1 AP to break line of sight — knock them out, close a door, take cover —
  before being shot at the start of their next look.
- **KO versus kill is a weighted resource decision, not a flavour choice.** The
  taser is limited-charge and slow to recharge; killing is free but raises the
  alarm and can cost +2 on the wrong guard. Francis's account of wasting his only
  taser charge and then having to kill someone he could have tased is the
  canonical demonstration.
- A KO'd guard can be found and **woken** by a colleague, restarting the threat.

## Incognita: a second economy braided into the first

**PWR** is the hacking currency, generated by programs (Power Drip +1/turn;
Emergency Reserve +5/turn when PWR < 3, three-turn cooldown) and spent breaking
firewalls (Lockpick 1.0: one firewall for 2 PWR; Lockpick 2.0: two for 3; Dagger:
three for 1; Parasite: one per turn at escalating cost per instance; Data Blast:
one firewall on everything within four tiles for 3).

**Firewalls** gate every mainframe device — doors, cameras, safes, drones,
turrets, augment grafters — and get +1 stronger at alarm level 2.

**Daemons** are the alarm reaching into the hacking game: randomised penalties
that attach to devices once the alarm crosses a threshold, countered only by
dedicated programs (Daemon Sniffer reveals one; Hunter strips one for 5 PWR).

Two economies, one escalation variable. You cannot out-hack the clock, because the
clock scales what hacking costs.

<https://invisibleinc.fandom.com/wiki/Comparison_of_hacking_programs>

## Extraction is a separate declared step

Leaving the level is not "walk off the edge." An elevator or designated exit is
"the only way to exit in one piece," and the objective (take the thing) and the
extraction (get every surviving agent to the exit and use it) are two distinct
success conditions. Because the alarm never stops climbing, every turn spent
looting after the objective is banked is pure risk against incremental reward.

## Campaign meta

- **72 in-fiction hours** frame the campaign; each mission consumes a chunk. The
  campaign has the same shape as one mission's alarm, one scale up.
- **Mission types are resource axes**, and the clock forces you to pick: corporate
  vaults (credits), detention centres (rescue and recruit agents), cybernetics
  labs (install augments, unlock slots — four max per agent), nanofab vestibules
  (a bigger shop). Money, team size, build depth, gear access — you cannot have
  all four.
- **Capture is soft permadeath.** A captured agent is *guaranteed* to be the
  rescue target of the next detention-centre mission. Skip it and they fall into
  the general recruitable pool — still recoverable, just not yours. Rescued agents
  keep augments but lose items. Capture costs tempo and a specialised build, not
  the run.
- The whole thing is tuned so the player is **always slightly behind**. It is not
  trying to let you catch up; it is trying to make the losing race feel earned.
