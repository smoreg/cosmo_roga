# The extraction loop as a genre

Research dump, 2026-09-09. Escape from Tarkov, Hunt: Showdown, Dark and Darker,
Helldivers 2, ARC Raiders, Deep Rock Galactic, Darktide.

## The contract

> Do you extract now with what you have, or push deeper for better loot? The
> deeper you go, the better the potential reward — and the higher the chance of
> losing everything.

<https://www.pcgamer.com/dark-and-darker-proves-extraction-shooters-dont-have-to-be-shooters-at-all/>

Dark and Darker is the proof the loop is genre-agnostic: same contract, fantasy
dungeon, classes as risk profiles. It is not about shooting.

## How each game softens total loss

**Tarkov** runs two separate mitigations that do different jobs.

- **The secure container** is an absolute carve-out: items inside never drop, ever.
  A designed safe slot so no run is catastrophic.
- **Insurance** is deliberately weak and probabilistic: pay a fee before the raid,
  and if you die *and your killer does not loot the corpse*, the item comes back
  after hours of delay (Prapor cheap and 12–20h; Therapist pricier and faster).
  The safety net is **tied to the threat itself** — whether you get your rifle back
  depends on the behaviour of the person who shot you.

<https://escapefromtarkov.fandom.com/wiki/Insurance>

**Hunt: Showdown** is the harsh end: a Hunter who dies, or fails to extract before
the timer, is gone forever with their gear and their levels. What survives is
account-level Bloodline XP and the unlocked weapon and trait pool. The lever is
**separating what you lose on death from what actually progresses you** — and that
separation is the genre's main anti-death-spiral device.

Its escalation is worth naming precisely: **the Banishing.** Claiming the boss
bounty triggers a visible burning effect broadcasting your exact position to every
other team for about two minutes. *Success is what makes you the target.* The
reward and the danger spike are the same event, which is why the extraction
decision — which exit, how fast — is forced rather than optional.

<https://huntshowdown.fandom.com/wiki/Bloodline> ·
<https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/HuntShowdown>

## Four shapes of exit pressure

| Shape | Example | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional deadline | ARC Raiders | A countdown, after which the map is bombed and everyone left dies regardless of gear or position. A slower electromagnetic storm also disables some exits, forcing a mid-run replan |
| The exit becomes the fight | Helldivers 2 | Calling the dropship starts a 2-minute timer, and the extraction zone's threat-generation radius (250 m, scaling as the timer runs) actively pulls enemies to your fixed position. Extraction is the climax, not a quiet dash |
| Compounding waves | Deep Rock Galactic, Point Extraction | Waves after an initial 55 s delay, shrinking 1 s per wave, never stopping |
| Success marks you | Hunt: Showdown | See the Banishing, above |

The Deep Rock case is the cautionary one. The design is deliberate, but players
report *"it's not communicated that you're on a timer"*, and falling behind the
wave cadence snowballs — punishing weak squads disproportionately. **An escalation
curve that is not legible in the UI reads as unfair rather than tense.** This is
the same lesson as ALARM → SECURITY LEVEL, arrived at from the opposite direction.

## What happens when you remove the humans

Two 2026 data points, both explicit PvE conversions:

**Darktide's Expeditions** — extraction grammar with the PvP stripped: 10–30
minute open-map runs scavenging Tech-Remnants, die or time out and "you get
nothing," with radiation and pollution instead of camping squads to force
movement. The developers' own framing: *"it's sort of like an extraction shooter
without the PvP."*

**ARC Raiders' Shared Watch** — a limited-time PvE mode, made in direct response
to community complaints about gear fear, keeping the storms and the orbital
bombardment as the pressure source.

What is lost is specific and worth naming: **the unpredictable adversarial layer**
— another squad looting the same exit, an ambush, a panicked third party into your
fight. Scripted and randomised spawns are learnable, and therefore get less tense
over time, which is precisely the opposite of what a human opponent does.

The compensating trick both DRG and Darktide use: without other players,
escalation has to come from **maths** (wave timers, spawn density, damage over
time) rather than **behaviour** — and the maths must be steeper *and* far better
telegraphed, because players cannot read a spawn curve the way they read
footsteps.

## Gear fear and the death spiral

**Gear fear** is the genre's own term for the loss aversion created by
bring-your-own-loadout risk. It is what makes loadout choice a decision rather
than a formality, and it is why every one of these games ships a cheap starter
loadout — so a wipe cannot end a player's ability to keep playing.

**The death spiral**: lose your good gear, bring worse gear, die more easily, lose
that too. The known answers:

- A persistent stash plus cheap starter kit plus a market to rebuild quickly
  (Tarkov).
- Make the thing you lose cheap and the thing that progresses you separate
  (Hunt).
- Keep the floor competitive. If the cheapest viable loadout is *nearly* viable,
  gear fear is a tension; if bottom-tier gear is unplayable against looted
  top-tier, losing your kit is a progression wall instead of a decision.
