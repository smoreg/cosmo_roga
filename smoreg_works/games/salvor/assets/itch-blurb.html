Your father's tug is the last hull on the list. Between here and it: a chain of dead ships, and one drone left on the rack.

You never go aboard yourself. The drone does — six bays of modules, walking a derelict compartment by compartment while the ship works out that something is moving in it. Strip what sells. Take the contracts the yard pays for: carry out a marked crate, hold a console for five turns, start all three of a hull's systems. Then get back to the airlock you came in by.

A ship that has noticed you does not simply get harder. It posts a machine where you have already walked, then sends one after you, then starts shutting bulkheads behind you — and at the top of the ladder it begins setting charges in its own compartments and counting down.

Drones are bought, not respawned. When the rack is empty and the account is empty, the voyage ends there.

A turn-based roguelike. Permadeath, no meta-progression, and a run is a seed and a list of keypresses — nothing carries between them.
